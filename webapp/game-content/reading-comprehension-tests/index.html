Test 1: The Amazing Human Heart

Science

Your heart is one of the hardest-working parts of your body. It is a muscle, and its job is to pump blood to every part of you. The heart is about the size of your fist, but it is very powerful. It beats around 100,000 times every day. That means it beats more than 35 million times in one year!

The heart has four small rooms inside it. These rooms are called chambers. Two chambers are on the top, and two are on the bottom. Blood moves through these chambers in a special order. First, blood that needs oxygen goes into the heart. Then the heart sends this blood to the lungs. In the lungs, the blood picks up oxygen. After that, the heart pumps the oxygen-rich blood to the rest of the body. This is why we say the heart and lungs work as a team.

When you exercise, your heart beats faster. This happens because your muscles need more oxygen when you run or jump. The heart works harder to send more blood, and more oxygen, to your muscles. That is why your heart beats fast after you run up the stairs or play sports.

Doctors say that exercise is good for the heart. When people exercise often, their heart becomes stronger, just like other muscles in the body. A strong heart can pump blood more easily. On the other hand, if a person never exercises, their heart may become weak over time. Eating healthy food, sleeping enough, and staying active all help keep the heart healthy for many years.

Scientists continue to study the heart because it is so important for life. Without a heart, no other part of the body could get the oxygen it needs to work.

~320 words

1. Literal Comprehension

1. How many chambers does the heart have?

2. What happens to the blood when it goes to the lungs?

2. Cause and Effect

1. Why does your heart beat faster when you exercise?

2. What can happen to a person's heart if they never exercise?

3. Inference

1. Why do you think the text compares the heart to "other muscles in the body"?

2. Why do scientists continue to study the heart, even though we already know a lot about it?

4. Vocabulary in Context

1. In the text, the word "pump" is used to describe what the heart does. Using the context, explain what "pump" means.

2. The text says a strong heart "can pump blood more easily." What does "easily" mean in this sentence?

5. Summary

Write a short summary of the text in 2 or 3 complete sentences.

Test 2: The Long Journey of Monarch Butterflies

Nature

Every year, millions of monarch butterflies travel from Canada and the United States to Mexico. This trip is called a migration. It is one of the longest migrations of any insect in the world. Some monarchs fly more than 4,000 kilometers to reach their winter home.

Monarch butterflies migrate because of the weather. In the fall, the days get shorter and colder in the north. Monarchs cannot survive freezing temperatures, so they fly south to find a warmer place. In Mexico, they gather in forests with mild weather, where they rest until spring.

What makes this journey even more amazing is that no single butterfly completes the whole trip alone. A monarch's life is short, so the butterflies that fly south are not the same ones that fly back north later. Instead, several generations are born along the way. Scientists still do not fully understand how new generations know exactly where to go, since they have never traveled there before. Many scientists believe monarchs use the position of the sun and the Earth's magnetic field to find their direction.

Sadly, the number of monarch butterflies has been dropping in recent years. One reason is the loss of milkweed plants, which are the only food that baby monarchs (caterpillars) can eat. When farmers and cities remove milkweed to build roads or grow crops, the caterpillars lose their food source. Because of this, fewer caterpillars survive to become butterflies.

People can help monarchs by planting milkweed in gardens and parks. Protecting the forests in Mexico where monarchs spend the winter is also important. Many scientists and volunteers now work together to protect this incredible journey for future generations.

~330 words

1. Literal Comprehension

1. How far do some monarch butterflies fly during their migration?

2. What is the only food that monarch caterpillars can eat?

2. Cause and Effect

1. Why do monarch butterflies fly south in the fall?

2. What happens to caterpillars when milkweed plants are removed?

3. Inference

1. Why is it surprising that new generations of butterflies know where to go, even though they have never made the trip before?

2. Why do you think scientists and volunteers are working together to protect monarchs?

4. Vocabulary in Context

1. The text says monarchs "gather in forests with mild weather." Using the context, what does "mild" mean?

2. The text uses the word "generations." Based on the context, explain what this word means.

5. Summary

Test 3: Robots That Help in Hospitals

Technology

In many hospitals today, robots are working alongside doctors and nurses. These robots are not like the robots in movies. They do not talk or think like humans. Instead, they are designed to do specific jobs that help hospitals run more smoothly.

One type of hospital robot delivers medicine and supplies. Instead of a nurse walking to a storage room, a small robot can carry the items through the hallways. The robot uses sensors to avoid people and walls. Because the robot handles this simple task, nurses have more time to take care of patients.

Another type of robot helps during surgery. These surgical robots are controlled by a doctor, who sits at a special computer and moves the robot's tiny arms. The robot's hands are more precise than human hands, so it can make very small, exact movements. This helps doctors perform difficult operations with smaller cuts. As a result, patients often recover faster and feel less pain after surgery.

Some hospitals also use robots to clean rooms. These cleaning robots use ultraviolet light to kill germs and bacteria. Because germs can make patients sicker, keeping rooms clean is extremely important. A robot can clean a room quickly and does not get tired, so it can work all day without stopping.

Not everyone agrees that robots are always a good idea in hospitals. Some people worry that robots are expensive and that machines could make mistakes. However, most experts believe that when doctors and robots work together, patients receive better and safer care. As technology improves, hospital robots are likely to become even more common in the future.

2. Cause and Effect

3. Inference

1. Why do you think some people worry about using robots in hospitals?

2. Why does the text say robots and doctors should "work together" instead of robots replacing doctors?

4. Vocabulary in Context

Test 4: The Town Where It Rains Fish

Unusual Events

In a small town called Yoro, in Honduras, something very strange happens almost every year. During a heavy storm, hundreds of small fish suddenly fall from the sky, along with the rain. This event is called "Lluvia de Peces," which means "Rain of Fish" in Spanish. It usually happens between May and July, during the rainy season.

The first time people reported this event was more than 100 years ago. After a strong storm, local people walked outside and found the ground covered with small, silver fish. Because this happened again and again over the years, the town of Yoro began to celebrate a festival every year to remember the strange event.

For a long time, people could not explain why fish were falling from the sky. Some people believed it was a miracle. Others thought strong winds from the ocean picked up the fish and carried them to Yoro. However, scientists later discovered a different explanation. They found that the fish do not actually come from the sky at all.

According to scientists, heavy rain floods small rivers and underground water channels near Yoro. Because of the flooding, fish that were living underground or in small streams are pushed up to the surface. The strong wind and rain then make it look like the fish are falling from the sky, when they are really being pushed out of the flooded ground. Even though there is a scientific explanation, many people in Yoro still enjoy thinking of it as a wonderful mystery.

Today, tourists travel to Yoro every year hoping to see the famous "fish rain" for themselves.

1. Literal Comprehension

1. What is the name of the event in Spanish, and what does it mean?

2. During which months does this event usually happen?

2. Cause and Effect

1. According to scientists, why do the fish appear on the ground after a storm?

2. Why did the town of Yoro start a yearly festival?

3. Inference

1. Why do you think some people still prefer to believe the event is a "miracle" instead of a scientific explanation?

2. Why do tourists probably want to visit Yoro during the rainy season and not during other times of the year?

4. Vocabulary in Context

1. The text says heavy rain "floods small rivers." Using the context, what does "floods" mean?

2. The text calls the event a "wonderful mystery." Based on the context, what does "mystery" mean?

Test 5: A Day in the Life of a Marine Biologist

Jobs and Careers

A marine biologist is a scientist who studies life in the ocean. This job can include studying fish, sharks, coral reefs, whales, or even tiny creatures that are too small to see without a microscope. Marine biologists want to understand how ocean animals live, what they eat, and how they behave.

A typical day for a marine biologist can be very different depending on the project. Some days are spent outdoors, near or in the water. A marine biologist might put on a diving suit and explore a coral reef to check its health. They might also travel on a boat to observe whales or collect water samples from different parts of the ocean.

Other days are spent indoors, in a laboratory or an office. After collecting information in the ocean, marine biologists need to study it carefully. They look at the samples under microscopes, write reports, and use computers to organize their data. Because of this, marine biologists must be comfortable with both physical fieldwork and detailed scientific research.

To become a marine biologist, a person usually needs to study biology or a related subject at university. Many marine biologists also need to learn how to scuba dive safely, since much of their work happens underwater. It is not always an easy job. The ocean can be dangerous, and some research trips can be long and tiring.

However, most marine biologists say their job is worth the challenges. They get to explore places that few people ever see, and their research helps protect ocean life. Because of climate change and pollution, marine biologists play an important role in helping keep the oceans healthy for the future.

Test 6: Plastic in Our Oceans

Environmental Issues

Every year, millions of tons of plastic end up in the ocean. Some of this plastic comes from beaches, rivers, and cities, where wind and rain carry trash into the water. Other plastic falls from boats or ships. Once plastic enters the ocean, it does not disappear quickly. In fact, some types of plastic can take hundreds of years to break down completely.

This plastic causes serious problems for ocean animals. Sea turtles sometimes eat plastic bags because they look similar to jellyfish, one of the turtles' favorite foods. Birds and fish can also swallow small pieces of plastic by mistake. Because plastic cannot be digested, it can make animals feel full even when they have not eaten real food. As a result, many animals become sick or die from starvation, even though their stomachs are full of plastic.

Another big problem is something called microplastics. These are extremely small pieces of plastic, often smaller than a grain of rice. Microplastics form when larger plastic items break into tiny pieces over time. Because they are so small, microplastics are very difficult to remove from the water. Fish often eat microplastics without knowing it, and these tiny pieces can travel up the food chain, from small fish to bigger fish, and even to humans who eat seafood.

Fortunately, people around the world are working to reduce plastic pollution. Many countries have banned single-use plastic bags and straws. Volunteers organize beach clean-ups to remove trash before it reaches the ocean. Scientists are also developing new materials that break down faster than regular plastic. Small actions, like using reusable bottles and bags, can also help reduce the amount of plastic that enters the ocean each year.

~340 words

1. Literal Comprehension

1. Why do sea turtles sometimes eat plastic bags?

2. What are microplastics?

2. Cause and Effect

1. Why can eating plastic cause an animal to die of starvation, even with a full stomach?

2. Why can microplastics eventually end up inside humans who eat seafood?

3. Inference

1. Why do you think microplastics are described as "very difficult to remove from the water"?

2. Why might small personal actions, like using a reusable bottle, actually make a difference for the ocean?

4. Vocabulary in Context

1. The text says plastic can "break down" over time. Using the context, what does "break down" mean?

2. The text mentions the "food chain." Based on the context, explain what this term means.

5. Summary

Grammar Section: Talking About the Future

This section reviews four ways to talk about the future in English.

1. Future Continuous — will be + verb-ing

We use this to talk about an action that will be in progress at a specific time in the future.

Example: At 8 p.m. tomorrow, I will be studying for the test.

2. Future Perfect — will have + past participle

We use this to talk about an action that will be finished before a specific time in the future.

Example: By next Friday, she will have finished the project.

3. Future Plans — going to + verb

We use this to talk about plans or intentions that are already decided.

Example: We are going to visit my grandmother this weekend.

4. Future Predictions — will / going to + verb

We use "will" for predictions based on opinion, and "going to" for predictions based on evidence we can see now.

Example: I think it will rain later. / Look at those clouds — it is going to rain!
